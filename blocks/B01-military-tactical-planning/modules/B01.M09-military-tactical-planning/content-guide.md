### Slide 6: Introduction to Military Tactical Planning (7 min)

- Tactical planning is typically not associated with cyber or information operations and rather is normally reserved for those conducting tactical ground operations.
- Considering this fact, it may seem unusual in a training centered around cyberspace to dive into topics like convoys and tactical ground operations.
- Remember that as uniformed service members, the better you can contextualize your actions within military planning fundamentals, the better you can communicate and transition between warfighting domains.

“War is nothing but a duel on a larger scale…imagining a pair of wrestlers. Each tries through physical force to compel the other to do his will.” – Carl Von Clausewitz

- **Show:** The two-panel image; emphasize "same process, different terrain."

### Slide 13: Tactical Tasks (4 min)

- **What are Friendly-Oriented Tactical Tasks?**
  - Friendly-Oriented Tactical Tasks are actions where the primary focus is on the movement or support of a friendly force.
  - Unlike enemy-oriented tasks that focus on destroying or defeating an adversary, these tasks are centered on the friendly force's status and relationship to the enemy, terrain, or another friendly unit.
  - For example, convoy missions are typically friendly-oriented, with the main goal being to deliver personnel or supplies to a destination.

### Slide 15: Tactical Tasks (7 min)

- **What are Terrain-Oriented Tactical Tasks?**
  - Terrain-Oriented Tactical Tasks are actions focused on controlling or influencing the physical environment.
  - The primary purpose of these tasks is to seize, retain, or deny the enemy access to specific terrain or a geographic area.
  - Unlike tasks that focus on a friendly or enemy force, the terrain itself is the objective.

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Show:** The figure on the slide.
- **Say:** "A deliberate defense: I have been given the terrain task of 'control' in order to protect friendly forces — that's a friendly task. While doing that, with the enemy nearby, I am also told to reconnoiter them — that is either a terrain or enemy task. Three tactical tasks, three orientations, one mission."
- **Do:** Think-pair-share: students define the orientations, discuss whether tasks can cross them, then offer one cyber example for each. Capture the strongest examples. Treat population-oriented tasks briefly; do not force a cyber analogy.
- **Ask (Q):** Define the four orientations. Can a mission hold more than one? Give one cyber example that is not forced.
- **Expected Response (A):** Enemy, terrain, friendly, population. Tasks can flow between orientations. Population-oriented examples stay brief; do not force a cyber analogy.

### Slide 26: Tactical Planning Process (1 min)

  - **BAMCIS** provides a foundational framework for military leadership and planning at the tactical level.

**Column titles:**

| Acronym | Step | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B | Begin the Planning | Starts upon receipt of a mission. Involves an initial assessment using the METT-TC framework. |
| A | Arrange Reconnaissance | Determine information gaps and plan to fill them through reconnaissance and surveillance. |
| M | Make Reconnaissance | Execute the reconnaissance plan to confirm or deny assumptions about the enemy and terrain. |
| C | Complete the Planning | Finalize the operational plan based on updated information gathered during reconnaissance. |
| I | Issue the Order | Clearly and concisely communicate the finalized plan to subordinate leaders. |
| S | Supervise | Oversee the execution of the plan, making adjustments as the situation evolves. |

### Slide 54: EMLCOA (7 min)

- After completing the METT-TC analysis, synthesize the findings to develop a comprehensive assumption of enemy actions. The EMLOCOA answers three questions:
  - **What is the enemy’s mission?**
      - **Example**: Is the enemy trying to achieve a specific tactical task like Exfiltrate sensitive data, or Disrupt a critical service?
  - **What is the enemy’s current tactical activity?**
      - **Example**: Are they staging tools for an attack? Conducting active reconnaissance? Maintaining persistence?
  - **What are the enemy’s actions on contact?**
      - **Example**: How will they react when we take action against them? Will they delete their presence, deploy ransomware, or escalate their activity?

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Say:** "EMLCOA is not a guess. It is the output of your METT-TC. You have done SALUTE, DRAW-D, and terrain — now you assemble an assumption of the enemy's plan. Think of it as their task, their purpose, and their method of imposing their will. That assumption is what your commander has to counter — and your scheme of maneuver has to counter it in two states: what they are doing right now, and what they will do the moment they realize we are here."
- **Show:** The three driving questions plus the definition.
- **Do:** Reminder: "Actions on contact is where DRAW-D pays off. If you skipped DRAW-D, you cannot answer question three. And remember — 'contact' does not mean a gunfight. It means any input from us that changes the enemy's state — a blocked account, a rotated credential, a sudden change in traffic. If they notice, that is contact."
- **Ask (Q):** What three questions drive EMLCOA, and what counts as contact in cyberspace?
- **Expected Response (A):** Enemy mission; current tactical activity; actions on contact. Contact is any input that alters their state, not only kinetic.

### Slide 58: Center of Gravity (7 min)

### Slide 63: The Exploitation Plan (7 min)

### Slide 67: Scheme of Maneuver, Fire Support Plan, and Tasks (7 min)

- **Scheme of Maneuver:**
  - Describes how the elements of the exploitation plan will be implemented in a coordinated manner, often through designated phases of the operation.
- **Fire Support Plan:**
  - A parallel concept in cyberspace operations.
  - Coordinates the use of rear echelon support (e.g., forensics, national agencies) with the tactical actions on the network.
  - Ensures actions are deconflicted and mutually supporting.
- **Tasks**:
  - Clear, concise tasking assigned to individual operators or teams.
  - Crucial for transitioning from a collection of individuals into a synchronized team executing a unified plan.
