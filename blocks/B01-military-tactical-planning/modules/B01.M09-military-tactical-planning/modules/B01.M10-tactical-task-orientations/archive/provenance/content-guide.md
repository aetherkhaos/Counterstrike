# Tactical-Task Orientations

This Module was previously an embedded exercise in [B01.M09 Military Tactical Planning](../B01.M09-military-tactical-planning/content-guide.md). Complete it in sequence immediately after that lecture.

### Slide 5: Tactical Task Orientations

- Four orientations for tactical tasks:
  - **Enemy-oriented** (e.g., reconnoiter, destroy, interdict, disrupt)
  - **Terrain-oriented** (e.g., control, seize, secure)
  - **Friendly-oriented** (e.g., protect, support, guard)
  - **Population-oriented** (actions focused on influencing or protecting a population)
- Tasks can flow between orientations — you can hold multiple at once
- Not every task maps cleanly to cyber; we will not force it

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Show:** Project `../../references/TacticalTasks.png` full-screen. Walk the matrix column by column; note that some source task matrices mark tasks that have more than one classification.
- **Say:** "A deliberate defense: I have been given the terrain task of 'control' in order to protect friendly forces — that's a friendly task. While doing that, with the enemy nearby, I am also told to reconnoiter them — that is either a terrain or enemy task. Three tactical tasks, three orientations, one mission."
- **Do:** Think-pair-share: students define the orientations, discuss whether tasks can cross them, then offer one cyber example for each. Capture the strongest examples. Treat population-oriented tasks briefly; do not force a cyber analogy.
### Slide 6: Translating Tactical Tasks into Cyberspace

- Some tasks translate cleanly (reconnoiter, disrupt, destroy)
- Some translate with work (seize, control)
- Some may not translate at all — that is fine
- **Group Exercise (now):** translate the tactical task sheet into cyber equivalents
- **Callback (later this week):** a second exercise maps MITRE **ATT&CK**, **D3FEND**, and **ENGAGE** techniques onto the tactical tasks where the translation holds

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Say:** "Here is the first group exercise. You will get a handout of tactical task definitions from Operational Terms and Graphics. Your job is to argue which tasks apply to cyber, which apply with modification, and which do not apply at all. There is no single right answer — the argument matters more than the verdict. We are also going to do a follow-up exercise later this week where we map ATT&CK, D3FEND, and ENGAGE techniques onto these tactical tasks — so keep your verdicts; we will come back to them."
- **Show:** Distribute the tactical-task handout sourced from `../../references/B130836 Operational Terms and Graphics.pdf`.
- **Do:** Break into groups of 3-4. Timebox: **20 minutes** of group work + **10 minutes** of back-brief. Collect verdicts on the whiteboard and tell the class you will return to them during the ATT&CK/D3FEND/ENGAGE block.
