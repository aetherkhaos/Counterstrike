# Lab L1.8 - Blank Student Rubric

**Student:** ______________________________  
**Team:** _________________________________  
**Instructor:** ____________________________  
**Date:** __________________________________

Use one copy per student. Each row uses fixed performance-level points: Poor = 1, Marginal = 2, Acceptable = 3, and Exemplary = 4. Record any row or category weighting in the placeholders below or in the LMS, but keep the rubric table at five columns. Suggested category weights are analytical rigor 25%, framework integration 25%, feasibility and discipline 20%, clarity and professionalism 15%, and teamwork 15%.

| Instructor question | Poor (1 pt) | Marginal (2 pts) | Acceptable (3 pts) | Exemplary (4 pts) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AR-1 (Weight: ___). Did the student ground mission analysis in the issued mission, commander’s intent, constraints, and scenario evidence?** | Missed task/purpose or mostly repeated scenario text without analysis. Score: 1 | Identified some tasks or constraints but left intent, assumptions, or evidence unclear. Score: 2 | Clearly separated explicit tasks, implied tasks, constraints, purpose, and key assumptions. Score: 3 | Restated mission and intent separately (purpose, key tasks, end state) and used that split to shape later planning and RFIs. Score: 4 |
| **AR-2 (Weight: ___). Did the student use SALUTE, DRAW-D, and OCOKA-W to explain the enemy and terrain?** | Used generic adversary language or listed hosts without doctrine-based reasoning. Score: 1 | Used some framework terms but with uneven evidence or limited terrain depth. Score: 2 | Applied SALUTE, DRAW-D, and friendly/enemy OCOKA-W with scenario-grounded findings. Score: 3 | Connected doctrine, evidence, terrain, tempo, and likely enemy adaptation into a coherent estimate. Score: 4 |
| **AR-3 (Weight: ___). Did the student justify EMLCOA, CG, and CV with evidence and uncertainty?** | EMLCOA, CG, or CV was missing, overbroad, or unsupported. Score: 1 | Produced plausible statements but weakly tied them to evidence or RFIs. Score: 2 | Produced coherent EMLCOA and actionable CG/CV with stated assumptions. Score: 3 | Explained why the CG/CV was the best planning focus and how uncertainty would affect the plan. Score: 4 |
| **FI-1 (Weight: ___). Did the student map adversary behavior to ATT&CK before selecting defensive or engagement options?** | Jumped to tools, defenses, or technique names without behavior evidence. Score: 1 | Mapped some behaviors but included unsupported or memorized technique choices. Score: 2 | Mapped major observed behaviors to ATT&CK techniques or clusters with evidence. Score: 3 | Prioritized mappings by mission impact and explained confidence, gaps, and next evidence needs. Score: 4 |
| **FI-2 (Weight: ___). Did the student pair D3FEND and ENGAGE options to the CV and desired effect?** | Listed framework labels without explaining purpose or effect. Score: 1 | Proposed options that were partially relevant but weakly connected to the CV. Score: 2 | Paired options to behavior and stated goal, effect, authority, and risk. Score: 3 | Built realistic option sets that exposed, affected, or elicited adversary behavior while preserving mission discipline. Score: 4 |
| **FI-3 (Weight: ___). Did the student's CAD graph support the operational argument?** | CAD graph was missing, disconnected, or graphically present but analytically empty. Score: 1 | Included required elements but with weak provenance, labels, or edge logic. Score: 2 | Included required node types, typed edges, provenance, and countermeasure relationship. Score: 3 | Used CAD to make the plan more defensible by clearly showing agent, attack, artifact, condition, and countermeasure relationships. Score: 4 |
| **FD-1 (Weight: ___). Did the student's options respect authority, risk, and operational safety?** | Recommended disruptive action without authority, risk, or adversary-reaction analysis. Score: 1 | Mentioned risk or authority but did not consistently apply it to options. Score: 2 | Identified authority, risk, operational safety, and adversary tip-off concerns for major options. Score: 3 | Balanced mission effect, evidence preservation, service continuity, and adversary reaction across the option set. Score: 4 |
| **FD-2 (Weight: ___). Did the scheme of maneuver directly target the critical vulnerability?** | SOM was missing, generic, or disconnected from the CV. Score: 1 | SOM had phases but weak logic between actions, CV, and desired effects. Score: 2 | SOM was phased and tied actions to the CV and commander intent. Score: 3 | SOM showed disciplined sequencing, branch considerations, and clear measures of performance/effectiveness. Score: 4 |
| **FD-3 (Weight: ___). Did the fire support plan and task list make execution feasible?** | Support needs, deconfliction, or role tasks were absent or vague. Score: 1 | Identified some support or tasks but without purpose, ownership, or measures. Score: 2 | Identified support, deconfliction, role tasks, purposes, and measures. Score: 3 | Produced a realistic handoff-ready execution structure with clear ownership, dependencies, supervision, and confirmation criteria. Score: 4 |
| **CP-1 (Weight: ___). Did the student communicate a commander-focused argument during briefs?** | Brief was unfocused, overly technical, or did not support a decision. Score: 1 | Brief conveyed some useful information but lacked prioritization or commander framing. Score: 2 | Brief was concise, mission-focused, and supported decisions with evidence. Score: 3 | Brief anticipated commander concerns and clearly explained risk, confidence, and recommended next actions. Score: 4 |
| **CP-2 (Weight: ___). Were written products clear, organized, and usable as handoff artifacts?** | Packet was incomplete, hard to follow, or missing core products. Score: 1 | Packet had most products but organization or traceability was inconsistent. Score: 2 | Packet was complete, readable, and traceable from evidence to plan. Score: 3 | Packet was polished enough for another shift to understand assumptions, evidence, decisions, and next steps without verbal explanation. Score: 4 |
| **CP-3 (Weight: ___). Did visual products clarify rather than distract from the plan?** | Visuals were missing, confusing, or unrelated to the operational argument. Score: 1 | Visuals existed but were crowded, inconsistent, or weakly explained. Score: 2 | Visuals highlighted key terrain, mappings, CAD relationships, and plan phases. Score: 3 | Visuals improved decision quality by making priorities, relationships, and risks immediately understandable. Score: 4 |
| **TW-1 (Weight: ___). Did the student make a visible individual contribution to the team product?** | Contribution was not visible in packet, brief, Q&A, or team note. Score: 1 | Contribution was limited, unclear, or mostly passive. Score: 2 | Contribution was visible and tied to a meaningful product, brief segment, or analysis role. Score: 3 | Contribution materially improved team analysis, product quality, or brief performance. Score: 4 |
| **TW-2 (Weight: ___). Did the student collaborate and adapt based on team or instructor feedback?** | Resisted coordination, duplicated effort, or ignored feedback. Score: 1 | Participated inconsistently or accepted feedback without clearly improving the product. Score: 2 | Coordinated with teammates and incorporated feedback into the plan. Score: 3 | Helped the team resolve disagreements, improve assumptions, and adapt the plan under time pressure. Score: 4 |
| **TW-3 (Weight: ___). Did the student handle Q&A and uncertainty professionally?** | Could not explain owned work or overstated unsupported claims. Score: 1 | Answered basic questions but struggled to separate facts, assumptions, and RFIs. Score: 2 | Answered questions with evidence, confidence, assumptions, and RFIs. Score: 3 | Used Q&A to strengthen the team's handoff by clarifying tradeoffs, risks, and evidence needs. Score: 4 |
