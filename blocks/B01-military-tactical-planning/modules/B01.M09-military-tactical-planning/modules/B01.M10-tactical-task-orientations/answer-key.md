# Tactical-Task Orientations — Instructor Answer Key

## Objective and starting context

Students name the four tactical-task orientations and notice that some verbs sit in more than one. Source: parent content guide slide 5 and the four task tables on the student deck (enemy, friendly, terrain, population).

## Expected product

A short oral or whiteboard answer: the four orientations, one example of a verb that crosses orientations, and one cyber example that is not forced.

## Acceptable answers

- Orientations: enemy, friendly, terrain, and population.
- Overlap is expected. The deck marks Breach, Influence, Reconnoiter, Contain, Control, Occupy, and Secure with an asterisk where a verb has more than one classification.
- A mission can hold more than one orientation at once. The dug-in defense example is the teaching case: control (terrain or friendly) plus reconnoiter (terrain or enemy).
- Population-oriented examples stay brief. Do not require a cyber analogy for Advise, Train, or Transition to civilian control.

## Grading checkpoints

- Accept: four orientations plus one honest overlap.
- Marginal: a verb list with no orientation.
- Do not accept: a claim that every population task has a clean cyber equivalent.
