### Slide 1: Why We Use a Fictional Scenario (3 min)

- A single, persistent, fictional setting for the whole course
- Lets us operate freely without classification or attribution issues
- Gives every block a **common target, terrain, and adversary**
- Both sides are deliberate caricatures of real-world archetypes — recognizable without being real

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** A stylized regional map graphic — two unnamed states in contrast (a large authoritarian state in muted reds/greys to the north-east; a smaller democratic state in blues/greens to the south-west), with a border line between them. No real-world place names. Clean, illustrative, suitable as a chapter opener.
- **Say:** "Before we get into the scenario itself — why fictional? Two reasons. First, classification: a fictional scenario keeps us out of problems during training. Second, common ground: every student, every block, every lab will share this world. When I say 'Varensk,' you will know what I mean for the rest of the course."
- **Show:** The regional map graphic.
- **Do:** Take a show of hands. The hands are the point.
- **Ask (Q):** Who has taken a course that used a real-world actor as the scenario and hit a classification wall mid-lab?
- **Expected Response (A):** Facilitation only. Hands, not a scored answer.

### Slide 2: The Conflict in Two Minutes (4 min)

- Two neighboring states; long shared history
- Post-imperial break-up: **Lunovia** pivoted West; **Varensk** sees that as betrayal
- Lunovia accuses Varensk of cyberattacks, disinformation, and separatist support
- Past 5 years: increasing military exercises near the border, major cyber operations against Lunovian energy and telecom
- International observers: **open conflict possible within 3-5 years**

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** A simple timeline graphic spanning "Imperial era" → "Break-up" → "Lunovia aligns West" → "Cyber & hybrid ops" → "Present (escalating)". Five tiles on a horizontal line.
- **Say:** "Here is the backstory in a paragraph. Two neighboring states, long shared history. When the old empire broke up, Lunovia went West. Varensk treats that as betrayal. Over the past five years, the border has heated up and Varensk has run cyber and hybrid operations against Lunovia's critical infrastructure. International observers expect open conflict in 3 to 5 years."
- **Show:** The timeline graphic.
- **Do:** Let the room call out parallels, then steer back to the scenario names.
- **Ask (Q):** What real-world parallels do you hear in this backstory?
- **Expected Response (A):** Facilitation only. Acknowledge parallels, then steer back: "We operate in Varensk vs. Lunovia for the rest of the course."

### Slide 3: Varensk — Core Identity (4 min)

- **~90 million** population; one-party presidential republic
- Capital: **Karsograd**
- **Authoritarian, centralized state** with a nationalist ideology — "Varensker Revivalism"
- President **Viktor Surnov** — in power 17 years
- Economy: natural gas, rare earths, heavy manufacturing
- Foreign policy: regional hegemony; cyber as a primary tool of influence

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** A country-profile card graphic. Left side: a stylized flag bar in muted reds and greys. Right side: a list of fact-sheet labels ("Population", "Capital", "Government", "Leader") — labels only, no values — for the lecturer to overlay. Do not put real text in the card so the visual is reusable.
- **Say:** "Varensk is the big one. Ninety million people, centralized authoritarian government, a nationalist ideology called Varensker Revivalism. The president has been in power 17 years. Economy runs on gas, rare earths, and heavy industry. Their foreign policy is regional dominance and they treat cyber as a primary lever."
- **Show:** The country-profile card.
- **Do:** Let the room answer — it seeds the cyber-apparatus discussion two slides from now.
- **Ask (Q):** If you were in their intelligence service, what would your job priorities be?
- **Expected Response (A):** Facilitation only. Steer toward influence, espionage, and infrastructure pressure. Do not require a unit name yet.

### Slide 4: Varensk — Geography and Strategic Posture (4 min)

- **Terrain:** northern tundra, central plains (Iron Steppe), eastern coastal marshlands
- Key features:
  - **Iron Steppe** — armored maneuver terrain
  - **Voryn Mountains** — natural southern barrier
  - **Karsenic Coast** — naval bases and **cyber-intelligence hubs**
- Strategic posture:
  - Pursues regional dominance
  - Large, well-funded cyber command integrated with intelligence services
  - Uses cyber for espionage, political pressure, and **infrastructure disruption**
  - Runs information operations to shape narratives in neighboring states

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide — the Varensk profile in Slide 3 is sufficient; this slide is content-heavy and benefits from room to read.
- **Say:** "Physical geography matters even in a cyber course. The Karsenic Coast hosts their naval bases *and* their cyber-intelligence hubs. That co-location is realistic — in the real world, signals and cyber infrastructure cluster near the sea and near the core."
- **Show:** The bullet list; linger on Karsenic Coast.
- **Do:** Tie back: "When we plan against Varensk in later blocks, 'location' on your SALUTE is going to point here."
- **Ask (Q):** When we plan against Varensk later, where does SALUTE location point on this map?
- **Expected Response (A):** Steer toward the Karsenic Coast (naval bases and cyber-intelligence hubs).

### Slide 5: Varensk Cyber Apparatus — Three Directorates (4 min)

- **Directorate 7 (D7)** — Military Cyber Operations
  - Destructive, battlefield-support, supply-chain, ICS/SCADA
- **External Intelligence Service (EIS)** — Foreign Espionage
  - Stealthy, long-dwell, cloud-identity focused
- **Internal Security Bureau (ISB)** — Domestic and Near-Abroad
  - Hack-and-leak, political interference, rapid-deploy malware

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** A three-column organizational diagram. Top bar: "Varensk Cyber Apparatus". Three columns below: "D7 — Military", "EIS — Foreign Espionage", "ISB — Domestic / Near-Abroad". Each column styled identically. Clean, neutral color palette.
- **Say:** "Varensk's cyber apparatus has three directorates. D7 is the military cyber arm — the destructive one. EIS is foreign espionage — the stealthy one. ISB is domestic and near-abroad — the loud, politically-driven one. When we plan against a specific actor in this course, you will trace them back to one of these three."
- **Show:** The three-column diagram.
- **Do:** Preview: the next slide breaks each directorate into specific units.
- **Ask (Q):** Which directorate is destructive, which is stealthy, and which is politically loud?
- **Expected Response (A):** D7 destructive; EIS stealthy; ISB politically loud / hack-and-leak.

### Slide 6: Varensk Cyber Units and Real-World Analogues (5 min)

- **D7 — Military**
  - Unit 7440 *Black Forge* — ICS/SCADA disruption (Havoc C2)
  - Unit 2213 *Iron Wolf* — destructive malware, supply-chain (Empire C2)
  - Unit 9931 *Storm Pike* — battlefield cyber/EW (Empire C2)
- **EIS — Foreign Espionage**
  - Group *Seraph-3* — stealthy cloud persistence (Cozy Bear analogue; Mythic C2)
  - Group *Lattice-9* — diplomatic / NGO espionage (Mythic C2)
- **ISB — Domestic / Near-Abroad**
  - Unit 18 *Amber Tide* — hack-and-leak, political ops (**Gamaredon analogue**; Metasploit Framework)
  - Unit 47 *Sable Net* — border-region cyber operations (Metasploit Framework)

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Say:** "Here is the unit-level detail. Pay attention to the C2 framework assignments — they are intentional and consistent through the course labs. Also note the real-world analogues in parentheses: *Seraph-3* plays the Cozy Bear role; *Amber Tide* plays the Gamaredon role. When we do the Gamaredon walk-through in Block 2 and the labs use *Amber Tide* instead — that is the reason."
- **Show:** The unit list; point specifically to Unit 18 *Amber Tide* and note its Gamaredon role.
- **Do:** Tell students to dog-ear this slide. They will return to it every time the course names a Varensk unit.
- **Ask (Q):** Which Varensk unit plays the Gamaredon role in this course, and which plays Cozy Bear?
- **Expected Response (A):** *Amber Tide* (Unit 18) = Gamaredon; *Seraph-3* = Cozy Bear.

### Slide 7: Lunovia — Core Identity (4 min)

- **~32 million** population; parliamentary democracy
- Capital: **Velystok**
- Prime Minister: **Alina Veskova**
- Younger, more urban, more globally connected than Varensk
- Diversified economy; **rapidly expanding digital infrastructure**
- Foreign policy: align with Western democracies, build cyber resilience, pursue digital sovereignty

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** A country-profile card matching Slide 3's layout, not a travel brochure. Left: a stylized flag bar in muted blues and greens. Right: fact-sheet labels only ("Population", "Capital", "Government", "Leader") with blank value lines. No climate, attractions, tourism, or "country name" placeholder map.
- **Say:** "Lunovia is the smaller, democratic state. Thirty-two million people, parliamentary republic, younger and more urban than Varensk. Their economy is diversified and *digital*. Their foreign policy is Western alignment, cyber resilience, digital sovereignty. In short — they look like the state we usually find ourselves defending."
- **Show:** The country-profile card.
- **Do:** Draw the parallel explicitly. Get a nod of understanding from the room.
- **Ask (Q):** When we are the blue team, who are we in this scenario? When we plan DCO-RA out of blue-space, who are we?
- **Expected Response (A):** Blue team = Lunovia. DCO-RA out of blue-space = a Lunovian unit.

### Slide 8: Lunovia — Geography and Strategic Posture (4 min)

- **Terrain:** rolling plains, fertile agricultural heartland, dense northern forests
- Key features:
  - **Lunovian Corridor** — flat, open — vulnerable to mechanized invasion
  - **Vel River** — powers hydro; runs through the capital
  - **Western Highlands** — natural defensive terrain
- Strategic posture:
  - Heavy investment in **defensive cyber**, especially critical infrastructure
  - Partnerships with foreign cybersecurity firms and allied intelligence sharing
  - Small but professional military; strong territorial defense doctrine
  - **Heavy cloud reliance** — efficient, but a disruption vector

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Say:** "Lunovia's strategic posture is exactly where a lot of real-world partner nations sit — heavy investment in defensive cyber, lots of allied partnerships, strong territorial defense, and a cloud-heavy digital government. That cloud reliance is efficient, but it is also an attack surface we will revisit."
- **Show:** The bullets; linger on the last sub-bullet (cloud reliance).
- **Do:** Capture two or three answers on the board.
- **Ask (Q):** Name one thing you would target first if you were Varensk and you wanted to rattle Lunovia without triggering a kinetic response.
- **Expected Response (A):** Facilitation only. Cloud, energy, telecom, or hydro on the Vel River are on-scenario. Do not grade a single correct target.

### Slide 9: Lunovia Cyber Defense Structure (4 min)

- **National Cyber Defense Agency (NCDA)**
  - Protects government networks; coordinates incident response; runs national CERT
- **Digital Infrastructure Protection Corps (DIPC)**
  - Guards energy, telecom, and transportation sectors; works with private industry
  - Includes a **Cyber Auxiliary Reserve** — volunteer experts activated in crises
- **Foreign Cyber Partnerships**
  - Intelligence sharing with allied democracies
  - Joint exercises and red-team exchanges

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** A three-pillar organizational diagram for Lunovia's defense, styled as a mirror of Slide 5's Varensk diagram. Pillars labeled "NCDA — National Cyber Defense Agency", "DIPC — Digital Infrastructure Protection Corps", and "Foreign Partnerships". Do not invent other expansions (not coastal defense, not intelligence planning command).
- **Say:** "Lunovia's defensive cyber structure has three pillars. NCDA runs the CERT and defends government networks. DIPC guards critical infrastructure sectors and includes a volunteer cyber auxiliary. And their foreign partnerships provide intelligence sharing and joint exercises. If you are playing blue, you are probably in NCDA or DIPC."
- **Show:** The three-pillar diagram.
- **Do:** Use the answers to set CPT assignment lanes (NCDA or DIPC).
- **Ask (Q):** Which Lunovian pillar would you most likely be assigned to as CPT operators?
- **Expected Response (A):** Facilitation only. NCDA or DIPC are the expected lanes from the Say script.

## Lesson summary

- The course uses one fictional setting so every block shares a common target, terrain, and adversary without classification problems.
- **Varensk** is the authoritarian adversary: three cyber directorates (D7, EIS, ISB) and named units with C2 assignments and real-world analogues (*Amber Tide* / Gamaredon; *Seraph-3* / Cozy Bear).
- **Lunovia** is the defender: NCDA, DIPC, foreign partnerships, and heavy cloud reliance. Blue team = Lunovia; DCO-RA out of blue-space = a Lunovian unit.
- The course network diagram is the terrain for planning, tradecraft, on-target, and agentic work.

## Questions and check on learning

- **Ask (Q):** Why is the scenario fictional?
- **Expected Response (A):** Classification / attribution safety, and a common target-terrain-adversary for every block.
- **Ask (Q):** Name Varensk's three cyber directorates and one unit you will see again.
- **Expected Response (A):** D7, EIS, ISB. Accept any listed unit; *Amber Tide* and *Seraph-3* are the analogues called out on Slide 6.
- **Ask (Q):** Which operational-environment elements on the Lunovian side will impact later planning?
- **Expected Response (A):** Steer toward cloud reliance, NCDA/DIPC structure, Lunovian Corridor / Vel River / Western Highlands, and the course network diagram as terrain.
