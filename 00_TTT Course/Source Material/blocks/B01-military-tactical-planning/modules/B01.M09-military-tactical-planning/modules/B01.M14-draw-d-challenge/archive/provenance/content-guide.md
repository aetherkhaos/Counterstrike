# DRAW-D Challenge

This Module was previously an embedded exercise in [B01.M09 Military Tactical Planning](../B01.M09-military-tactical-planning/content-guide.md). Complete it in sequence immediately after that lecture.

### Slide 16: Enemy Analysis — DRAW-D

- **D**efend — under what conditions can and will they defend?
- **R**einforce — under what conditions can and will they reinforce?
- **A**ttack — capability, limits, and conditions for attack
- **W**ithdraw — the critical one for counter-strike planning
- **D**elay — conditions under which they stall you

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Say:** "After SALUTE you move to DRAW-D: capabilities and limitations. For each action, answer three questions — can they do it, what limits them, and under what conditions will they do it? Withdraw is the one I want you to really sit with. If your adversary withdraws the instant they sense us, reconnoiter as a mission is gone."
- **Show:** The DRAW-D bullets; spend extra time on **Withdraw**.
- **Do:** Pose the scenario: "Your tasking is 'reconnoiter.' Your adversary's withdrawal condition is 'any anomaly in normal traffic.' What does that do to your scheme of maneuver?" Let the room answer.
### Slide 17: Group Discussion — DRAW-D on a Real Actor

- Pick one actor from the SALUTE exercise
- Produce a best-effort DRAW-D
- Identify where you had to guess
- Those guesses become **RFIs** to your intel section

**Instructor Notes:**

- **Image Prompt:** No image required for this slide.
- **Say:** "A big part of this is learning to recognize the questions you cannot answer from a public report. Those questions become RFIs. Your intel shop needs you to be explicit about what you need — 'tell me more about the adversary' is not a request they can action."
- **Show:** The same reports from Slide 11.
- **Do:** Timebox: **15 minutes**. Capture RFIs on the whiteboard; return to them at the end of the day.
