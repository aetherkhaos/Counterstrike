# Lab L1.8 - Week 1 Group Assessment
## Student Brief

**Sequence:** B01.M36, culminating assessment after all M01 instruction and activities.

**Duration:** Full training day, approximately 6-7 hours of work plus two 10-minute team briefs.

**Primary system:** PCTE live lab environment required; use the exercise support files in this folder, previously issued course handouts, whiteboard space, and team notes.

**Stack:** Exercise threat intelligence, Lunovia network map, Week 1 planning frameworks, ATT&CK, D3FEND, ENGAGE, D3FEND CAD, and team briefing materials.

## Operational Context

The commander has tasked your team to conduct DCO-IDM and DCO-CounterStrike operational planning for the Lunovia Network against a persistent Varenskian adversary. The network is currently compromised and is "bleeding" intelligence. Your job is to produce a commander-ready plan that denies or degrades the adversary's ability to collect intelligence while preserving friendly freedom of maneuver and generating viable options for pursuit.

This is the M01 culminating assessment. It requires you to bring together the
full module planning chain: tactical tasks, METT-TC, SALUTE, DRAW-D, OCOKA-W,
EMLCOA, center of gravity, critical vulnerability, exploitation planning,
ATT&CK, D3FEND, ENGAGE, CAD graphing, scheme of maneuver, fire support
planning, and role-based tasks.

Due to opsec concerns, the only AI tooling that you are authorized to use are locally hosted models.

## Scenario Inputs

At the start of the assessment, your team will receive or be directed to:

- [Threat Actor Intelligence Sheet - Amber Tide](supporting-documents/L1.8%20Threat%20Actor%20Intelligence%20Sheet%20-%20Amber%20Tide.md), describing observed TTPs, tooling, infrastructure, and behavior.
- [Lunovia Network Map](supporting-documents/L1.8%20Lunovia%20Network%20Map.png), showing the assessment network, observed activity, and available defensive evidence.

Treat missing details as RFIs. Do not invent certainty where the source material does not support it.

Previously issued lectures, framework handouts, and general scenario background remain valid references, but they are not part of this exercise support packet.

## Assessment Structure

The assessment has two phases. Each phase ends with a 10-minute team brief.

### Phase 1: METT-TC to Critical Vulnerability

**Time allotted:** Approximately 3 hours, including analysis and brief preparation.

Produce one consolidated team packet that includes:

- Mission analysis with explicit tasks, implied tasks, constraints, and purpose.
- Enemy analysis using SALUTE and DRAW-D.
- Troops and fire support available.
- Friendly and enemy OCOKA-W across physical, logical, and persona layers where relevant.
- Time, space, logistics, and civilian considerations.
- One-paragraph EMLCOA.
- Center of gravity and critical vulnerability statements.
- Key assumptions and RFIs.

Your Phase 1 brief should convince the commander that your analysis of the situation is sound before your team moves into options development.

MITRE ATT&CK T-Codes are required. Include them in your SALUTE, OCOKAW, and anywhere else that is relevant. All identified T-Codes need to be backed by evidence from the compromised environment (filepaths, hashes, logs, etc.)

### Phase 2: Exploitation Plan to Tasks

**Time allotted:** Approximately 2.5-3 hours, including CAD work and brief preparation.

Add to your Phase 1 packet:

- Exploitation plan mapping key ATT&CK techniques or behavior clusters to paired D3FEND and ENGAGE options.
- D3FEND CAD graph with at least one Agent node, two to three Attack nodes, one Artifact, one Condition, and one or more Countermeasures using typed edges and provenance.
- Scheme of maneuver that directly targets the critical vulnerability.
- Fire support plan identifying rear-echelon, higher, adjacent, and partner support requirements.
- Specific tasks for team members or functional roles, each with purpose, measures of performance, measures of effectiveness, and supervision plan.

Your Phase 2 brief should be a formal commander brief that explains how the plan will stop the bleeding without sacrificing discipline, evidence, or operational safety.

## Lab Objectives

You will:

1. Apply the full Week 1 tactical planning process to a realistic defended-network scenario.
2. Build an evidence-grounded enemy estimate from threat intelligence and network terrain.
3. Identify an actionable center of gravity and critical vulnerability.
4. Convert observed adversary behavior into ATT&CK-informed planning options.
5. Pair D3FEND and ENGAGE options with clear purpose, authority, risk, and expected effect.
6. Produce a CAD graph that supports, rather than replaces, the operational argument.
7. Develop a scheme of maneuver, fire support plan, and role-based tasks suitable for commander review.
8. Brief the plan clearly and professionally as a team.

## Constraints and Handling Rules

- Do not execute actions on live systems during this assessment.
- Do not assume authority for disruptive actions. State required authorities and risks for each option.
- Do not select countermeasures before explaining the adversary behavior and critical vulnerability they target.
- Do not overstate visibility. If evidence is incomplete, mark the gap as an RFI.
- Keep the commander audience in mind: concise, evidence-based, mission-focused, and decision-oriented.
- Separate what you know, what you assess, what you recommend, and what remains uncertain.
- Every team member must contribute visibly to the packet, brief, Q&A, or handoff products.

## Specific Tasks

1. **Mission analysis.**
   - Restate the mission in your own words.
   - Identify explicit tasks, implied tasks, constraints, and purpose.
   - Write task-purpose statements using "in order to" language.

2. **Enemy and terrain analysis.**
   - Complete SALUTE and DRAW-D using scenario evidence.
   - Produce friendly and enemy OCOKA-W.
   - Identify key terrain across physical, logical, and persona layers.
   - Record RFIs that would materially change the plan.

3. **EMLCOA, center of gravity, and critical vulnerability.**
   - Draft one enemy scheme of maneuver.
   - State the enemy's likely tactical task and purpose.
   - Describe likely enemy actions on contact.
   - Identify the CG/CV pair and explain why it is actionable.

4. **ATT&CK, D3FEND, ENGAGE, and CAD.**
   - Map major observed behaviors to ATT&CK techniques or behavior clusters.
   - Pair each major behavior with D3FEND and ENGAGE options.
   - State the intended effect, risk, authority, and evidence requirement for each option.
   - Build a CAD graph that shows the relationship between adversary action, artifact, condition, and countermeasure.

5. **Execution planning.**
   - Build a phased scheme of maneuver that targets the CV.
   - Identify fire support and deconfliction requirements.
   - Assign role-based tasks with purpose, measures, and supervision.
   - Prepare the commander brief and final packet.

## Success Criteria

- The packet shows disciplined use of Week 1 doctrine and source evidence.
- The EMLCOA, CG, and CV form a coherent operational argument.
- ATT&CK, D3FEND, ENGAGE, and CAD products support the plan instead of becoming disconnected framework artifacts.
- The plan respects authority, risk, operational safety, and the need to avoid spooking the adversary prematurely.
- The brief is concise, visual, commander-focused, and suitable for handoff to the next shift.
- Team participation is visible and balanced.
