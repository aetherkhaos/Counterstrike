# L1.8 Threat Actor Intelligence Sheet - Amber Tide

## Overview

Unit 18 "Amber Tide" is a Varensk-aligned advanced persistent threat group operating under the Internal Security Bureau (ISB) of Varensk. It is primarily focused on cyberespionage against Lunovian governmental institutions and has been active since at least 2013.

Amber Tide is currently the most active Varenskian threat actor in Lunovia. It focuses mainly on governmental entities, with limited spearphishing campaigns also observed against governmental institutions in several Lunovian allied partner nations.

A partner threat-hunting team recently detected the actor in a government network, observing aggressive mass lateral movement executed through PowerShell and VBScript scripts originating from many host computers. The group does not prioritize stealth. It compensates with frequent updates, changing obfuscation, and simultaneous deployment of multiple simple downloaders or backdoors to maintain access.

## Goals

- Exfiltrate files with specific extensions, including documents, archives, and credentials, from Lunovian governmental systems.
- Maintain persistent access to compromised Lunovian governmental networks through layered tooling.
- Facilitate lateral movement and new victim acquisition through weaponized Word documents and USB drives shared within or outside organizations.

## TTPs

| Category | Observed behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Initial access | Spearphishing with LNK/HTA attachments in archives, HTML smuggling, weaponized Microsoft Word documents/templates, and USB drive weaponization for replication. |
| Execution and lateral movement | PowerShell and VBScript command execution, WMI for USB discovery, Metasploit Framework integration, and mass internal propagation. |
| Persistence | Registry Run keys, Startup folder, logon scripts, Office template macros, scheduled tasks, and fileless registry storage. |
| Defense evasion | Command and HTML smuggling, embedded payloads, encrypted files, masquerading with double extensions and benign registry names, `mshta.exe` proxying, hidden files, AMSI bypass, and indicator removal. |
| Discovery and collection | Targeted file discovery on local, network, and USB drives; browser credential and cookie theft; screen capture; and data staging. |
| Command and control | HTTP/HTTPS with dynamic IP resolution, third-party services, cloud tunneling, proxies, reverse SOCKS, and frequent domain/IP rotation. |
| Exfiltration | Exfiltration over command-and-control channels after staging. |

## Tools

| Tool family | Assessment-relevant notes |
| --- | --- |
| Downloaders and loaders | Custom PowerShell and VBScript downloaders support encrypted PE files, PowerShell commands, and VBScript payloads. Payloads are often XOR-encrypted with the volume serial number as a key. |
| Weaponizers and droppers | USB-drive weaponizers create enticing LNK files with double extensions and conspicuous filenames. Document and template weaponizers use malicious remote references or macros. |
| Stealers and backdoors | File stealers target specific extensions. Additional tooling includes browser credential/cookie stealers, app-specific stealers, screen capture, remote shells, and reverse SOCKS proxies. |
| Support tooling | Metasploit Framework integration, utilities for cloud transfers, and additional payload delivery. |
| Obfuscation and evasion | Multi-layer string splitting and concatenation, base64, random variable and function names, registry-based fileless storage, and environmental keying. |

## Recent Campaign Pattern

- **Mid-2022 - Lunovian governmental spearphishing:** LNK/HTA attachments in archives led to compromise of a ministry network. Weaponized documents enabled lateral spread, and file stealers exfiltrated sensitive correspondence.
- **Late 2022 - Allied partner probe:** Spearphishing against allied governmental institutions delivered HTA-based downloaders. Persistence mechanisms were deployed, but no confirmed breach was reported.
- **Q2 2023 - Email server lateral movement:** A compromised Lunovian governmental email server served as a launch point for mass PowerShell/VBScript propagation across more than 50 endpoints. Credential and document exfiltration occurred before detection.
- **Q4 2023 - USB-driven internal spread:** Weaponized USB drives in a Lunovian governmental agency facilitated lateral movement. Stealers captured configuration files and screenshots before cleanup.

## Planning Implications

- Amber Tide's center of gravity is likely tied to rapid collection tempo, repeated access, and the ability to stage and move documents before defenders turn detection into action.
- Likely critical vulnerabilities include reliance on observable scripting behavior, user-writable staging locations, removable-media or user-driven access paths, and outbound web paths that can be correlated with host activity.
- Defensive options should account for adversary adaptation. If pressured, Amber Tide is likely to shift infrastructure, reattempt access, reinforce through alternate payloads, or accelerate collection rather than immediately abandon the mission.
