### 1.3 Terminal and enabling learning objectives

- **TLO-1:** At the end of the Counterstrike Operator Course, students will possess the ability to conduct military tactical planning in the context of DCO-RA
- **ELO-1:** Understand the process of conducting a METT-TC (Mission, Enemy, Terrain and Weather, Troops and Fire Support Available, Time, and Civil Considerations) analysis, and be able to recall and apply each component in a given scenario.
- **ELO-2:** Comprehend the steps involved in conducting a DRAW-D (Direction, Rally point, Alternate rally point, Withdrawal route, Designated formations) analysis, and be able to remember and implement each step during planning.
- **ELO-3:** Identify an adversary’s COG (Center of Gravity), understand its significance, and be able to recall the process of determining it in different situations.
- **ELO-4:** Study and understand how to determine an adversary’s critical vulnerabilities, and be able to recall and explain how these vulnerabilities can be exploited.
- **ELO-5:** Learn the process of determining EMLCOA (Enemy's Most Likely Course of Action), understand its importance, and be able to recall and apply this process in various scenarios.
- **ELO-6:** Recall the components of an effective exploitation plan.
- **ELO-7:** Comprehend the concept of a Scheme of Maneuver, learn how to develop one, and be able to recall and present its key aspects.
- **ELO-8:** Determine an adversary’s mission, understand its importance, and be able to recall and explain the mission in different contexts.
- **ELO-9:** Understand the purpose and components of OCOKA-W (Observation and fields of fire, Cover and concealment, Obstacles, Key terrain, Avenues of approach, and Weather), learn how to analyze these factors, and be able to recall and interpret the results.
- **ELO-88:** Given a threat report, create a SALUTE report and identify information gaps as requests for information.

### 4.3 Lesson summary

- Tactical planning applies the same small-unit rigor to cyberspace: doctrine first, then cyber translation.
- BAMCIS is the teaching spine; the tactical planning process (METT-TC → EMLCOA → COG / CV / exploitation plan → SOM → FSP → tasks) lives in Begin and Complete.
- Nested drills practice orientations, task translation, SALUTE, DRAW-D, OCOKA-W, and the COG chain (`B01.M10`–`B01.M17`). Apply the same chain to Varensk/Lunovia and leave an ATT&CK-ready handoff.
- Keep evidence, assumptions, and RFIs distinct. Explain the plan with task and purpose.

### 4.4 Questions and check on learning

- **Ask (Q):** What is the tactical planning pipeline we will reuse for the rest of this Block?
- **Expected Response (A):** METT-TC → EMLCOA → COG / CV / exploitation plan → scheme of maneuver → fire support plan → tasks.
- **Ask (Q):** How do SALUTE and DRAW-D differ, and what do gaps become?
- **Expected Response (A):** SALUTE is composition, disposition, and strength. DRAW-D is capabilities, limitations, and conditions — especially withdraw. Gaps become RFIs.
- **Ask (Q):** What is a center of gravity versus a critical vulnerability?
- **Expected Response (A):** CG is what lets the enemy execute the mission; CV is the exploitable lever on that CG. If you cannot name a CV, the CG is too abstract.
