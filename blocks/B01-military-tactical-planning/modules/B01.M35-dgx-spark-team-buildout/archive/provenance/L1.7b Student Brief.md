# DGX Spark Team Buildout
## Student Brief

**Sequence:** B01.M35, after B01.M34 and before B01.M36.

**Duration:** Full lab block, approximately 4-5 hours hands-on as a team.

**Primary system:** One factory-fresh NVIDIA DGX Spark per team, plus a team
workstation for browser and SSH access.

**Stack:** Docker Compose, Traefik, vLLM (two instances), LiteLLM, Open WebUI,
a local ACME CA, and an approved external `genai.mil` model. n8n is out of scope
for this lab and is covered later.

## Operational Context

In L1.7a you ran a single local model behind a chat UI on a workstation. A CPT
that wants shared, governed AI inference needs more than that: a hardened
gateway, multiple models behind one API, TLS with real certificates, and a
supply chain that works inside a closed network.

Your team has been issued a factory-fresh DGX Spark and told to turn it into a
self-hosted, team-shared AI service. You will build the stack one component at a
time, proving each piece works before adding the next. Everything you need -
container images, model weights, DNS, and certificates - is served from the
classroom Traefik and mirrors stacks the instructor is running. Nothing in this lab should be
pulled across the internet except your Stage 7 `genai.mil` model.

The mission outcome is operational fluency: by the end, any team member should be
able to explain what each component does, how requests flow from the browser to a
model, where TLS is terminated and trusted, and how a new model becomes available
to users without rebuilding the UI.

## Starting Assumptions

Your team begins with:

- A DGX Spark **in the box** (not yet assumed online). Expect a factory image in
  most cases; if the last return left the host in a bad or mid-lab state, you
  may need to reinstall the factory image before building.
- A team workstation with a browser, an SSH client, and administrator rights to
  install a certificate.
- Your assigned team domain (for example `team1.cso.ops`) and DGX IP (used after
  you bring the box up on the classroom network).
- Classroom network access for the DGX and lab workstations used in this build
  (use the path the instructor directs for this offering).
- The instructor classroom stacks online: Traefik (DNS, ACME CA, trust portal)
  and mirrors (container registry `registry.cso.ops`, model mirror
  `models.cso.ops`), with the lab root CA download at
  `http://trust.cso.ops/root_ca.crt` plus matching `root_ca.fingerprint`.
- This student guide (create every directory and Compose/config file as directed).

If any of these are missing at the start, notify the instructor before building.

## Access Method

1. Unbox, power up, confirm or restore the factory image, and bring the DGX onto
   the classroom network (console/local setup as needed), using the connectivity
   path the instructor specified.
2. **Primary remote method:** SSH from your team workstation to the DGX using
   your issued credentials/key. Run all build and `docker compose` commands
   there.
3. **Fallback (only if SSH is unavailable):** work at the DGX console directly.

Confirm where you are before you change anything:

```bash
hostname
whoami
pwd
```

Every member must be able to explain the whole request path, not just their part.

## Lab Objectives

You will:

1. Perform initial setup of a factory-fresh DGX Spark for the lab (DNS, CA trust,
   local registry/mirror configuration, GPU/runtime readiness).
2. Stand up `vllm-primary` and confirm it with a raw `curl` request.
3. Deploy Open WebUI.
4. Deploy the LiteLLM gateway pointed at `vllm-primary`.
5. Reconfigure Open WebUI to reach models through LiteLLM.
6. Add `vllm-secondary`, expose both models through LiteLLM, and show that Open
   WebUI picks up the new model automatically.
7. Generate a `genai.mil` API token and add the external Gemini Enterprise model
   to LiteLLM as a third model alongside the two local ones.

## Constraints and Handling Rules

- Pull all images and models from the classroom stack, not the internet. If a
  pull tries to reach the internet, stop and re-check your configuration.
- Do not paste real operational data, credentials, or sensitive material into any
  model. Use only lab-safe prompts.
- Treat the `genai.mil` token as sensitive: do not print it, commit it, screenshot
  it, or share it between teams.
- Bind application ports to loopback on the DGX; expose services only through
  Traefik, as your Compose file will do.
- Change the default `LITELLM_MASTER_KEY` before you expose the gateway - that
  key is what authenticates every client to LiteLLM.
- Grow Compose only with services you have reached in the guide. Prove each stage
  works before starting the next. A later stage failing usually means an earlier
  assumption was wrong.

## Specific Tasks

Work through the seven stages in [L1.7b Student Step-by-Step Guide.md](L1.7b%20Student%20Step-by-Step%20Guide.md).
The guide embeds the file contents you must create. Reason about what each
component needs before you wire it in; grow Compose service by service.

1. **Initial DGX setup.** Confirm the OS/GPU/runtime, point the box at lab DNS,
   install and verify the lab root CA, and configure Docker/Hugging Face to use
   the local registry and mirror.
2. **Primary inference.** Bring up `vllm-primary` and validate `/health` and a
   chat/completions request with `curl` before touching any UI.
3. **Chat UI.** Deploy Open WebUI and confirm it loads over TLS at your team
   domain.
4. **Gateway.** Deploy LiteLLM in front of `vllm-primary` and validate the
   unified OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
5. **Wire the UI to the gateway.** Point Open WebUI at LiteLLM and confirm chat
   works through the gateway.
6. **Second model + auto-update.** Add `vllm-secondary`, expose both models in
   LiteLLM, and demonstrate Open WebUI's model list updating without a UI rebuild.
7. **External model.** Generate a `genai.mil` token, add Gemini Enterprise to
   LiteLLM, and confirm all three models are selectable.

## Deliverables

Submit one team packet:

1. An architecture diagram of your final stack showing every component and the
   request path from browser to each model, with where TLS is terminated and
   where the lab CA is trusted.
2. Stage evidence (redact any token): the `vllm-primary` `curl` health/inference
   result; the LiteLLM `/v1/models` listing after Stages 4, 6, and 7; an Open
   WebUI screenshot showing the model list after Stage 6.
3. A short written explanation of how Open WebUI's model list stays in sync with
   LiteLLM, and why that matters operationally.
4. A one-paragraph reflection: for a CPT, what does this team-shared pattern give
   you over the single-workstation stack from L1.7a, and what new risks does it
   introduce (trust, secrets, availability)?
5. A brief note on how the team divided and completed the work.

## Success Criteria

- The DGX resolves lab names, trusts the lab CA, and pulls only from the local
  registry/mirror.
- `vllm-primary` answered a raw `curl` request before any UI existed.
- Traefik serves each service over HTTPS with a certificate the workstation
  trusts (no browser warning).
- LiteLLM exposes both local models, and Open WebUI shows the new model without a
  rebuild.
- A third, external `genai.mil` model is reachable through the same gateway.
- No `genai.mil` token appears in any deliverable.
- Every team member can explain the full request path.
