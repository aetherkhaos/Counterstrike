# OCOKA-W Analysis — Instructor Answer Key

## Objective and starting context

Teams fill friendly and enemy OCOKA-W. The student deck leaves the table blank. Source: content guide slides 19 through 21 and the worked terrain on slide 31. The two doctrinal sketches are the physical-terrain picture, not the cyber answer.

## Expected product

A two-column table: observation and fields of fire, cover and concealment, obstacles, key terrain, avenues of approach, weather. Both perspectives.

## Acceptable answers

From the worked FSB example:

- Friendly observation: endpoint telemetry, event logs, and network-boundary visibility. Treat each visibility claim as an assumption until confirmed.
- Enemy key terrain: user workstations, file servers, persistence locations, and document access. Not "the domain controller" by default.
- Avenues: spearphishing, weaponized removable media, and LNK execution.
- Obstacles differ by side. Friendly obstacles are gaps in logging, encryption, and authority. Enemy obstacles are endpoint detection, allow-listing, and segmentation when the terrain actually has them.
- Weather: physical weather only when it affects power or communications. Do not invent a cyber weather metaphor the team cannot tie to the mission.

## Grading checkpoints

- Accept: both perspectives and at least one key-terrain line that is not a default host.
- Do not accept: a host inventory labeled "terrain."
